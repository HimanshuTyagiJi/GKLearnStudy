    Mathematics Formulas

    Mathematics is the language of the universe. From simple arithmetic to complex calculus, formulas are the tools that help us describe relationships, solve problems, and understand patterns. Below is a selection of fundamental formulas from various branches of mathematics.

    Quadratic Formula

    For an equation ax² + bx + c = 0, the solutions for x are given by:
    x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a

    Definition: This formula provides the roots or solutions of a quadratic equation. The term inside the square root, (b² - 4ac), is called the discriminant, which determines the nature of the roots (real, complex, or repeated).
    Example: Solve x² + 5x + 6 = 0. Here, a=1, b=5, c=6.
    x = [-5 ± √(5² - 4*1*6)] / (2*1)
    x = [-5 ± √(25 - 24)] / 2
    x = [-5 ± √1] / 2
    So, x = (-5 + 1) / 2 = -2 and x = (-5 - 1) / 2 = -3.

    Pythagorean Theorem

    In a right-angled triangle: a² + b² = c²

    Definition: The square of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle, c) is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides (a and b).
    Example: If a side `a` = 8 and the hypotenuse `c` = 10, find the other side `b`.
    8² + b² = 10²
    64 + b² = 100
    b² = 100 - 64 = 36
    b = √36 = 6.

    Area of a Circle

    A = πr²

    Definition: This formula calculates the area (A) of a circle based on its radius (r), which is the distance from the center to any point on the edge. π (pi) is a constant approximately equal to 3.14159.

    Slope of a Line

    m = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁)

    Definition: The slope (m) of a line measures its steepness and is calculated as the change in the vertical coordinate (rise) divided by the change in the horizontal coordinate (run) between any two distinct points on the line.

    Physics Formulas

    Physics seeks to understand the fundamental principles of the universe, from the smallest subatomic particles to the largest galaxies. Formulas in physics are concise expressions of these principles, allowing us to predict the behavior of physical systems.

    Newton's Second Law of Motion

    F = ma

    Definition: The force (F) acting on an object is equal to the mass (m) of the object multiplied by its acceleration (a). This is a cornerstone of classical mechanics.
    Example: If a 10 kg object accelerates at 2 m/s², the force is F = 10 kg * 2 m/s² = 20 Newtons.

    Kinetic Energy

    KE = 1/2 * mv²

    Definition: Kinetic energy (KE) is the energy an object possesses due to its motion. It is determined by the mass (m) of the object and the square of its velocity (v).
    Example: A 2 kg ball moving at 3 m/s has KE = 0.5 * 2 * (3)² = 9 Joules.

    Ohm's Law

    V = IR

    Definition: A fundamental principle in electricity, Ohm's law states that the voltage (V) across a conductor is directly proportional to the current (I) flowing through it, where R is the constant of proportionality, known as resistance.

    Chemistry Formulas

    Chemistry is the study of matter and the changes it undergoes. Chemical formulas and equations are symbolic representations of molecules, compounds, and their interactions during chemical reactions, forming the basis of our understanding of the material world.

    Ideal Gas Law

    PV = nRT

    Definition: This equation describes the state of a hypothetical ideal gas. It relates the pressure (P), volume (V), number of moles (n), and temperature (T) of the gas. R is the ideal gas constant.

    Molar Mass Calculation

    Molar Mass = Mass / Moles

    Definition: Molar mass is a physical property defined as the mass of a given substance (chemical element or chemical compound) divided by the amount of substance (moles). It connects the macroscopic property of mass to the microscopic property of moles.

    Density Formula

    ρ = m / V

    Definition: Density (ρ) is a measure of mass (m) per unit of volume (V). It's a fundamental property of matter that describes how tightly packed its constituent particles are.

    Conversion Formulas

    Unit conversion is a fundamental skill in science and daily life. These formulas allow you to switch between different measurement systems, such as metric and imperial, ensuring consistency and accuracy in your calculations.

    Temperature Conversion (Celsius to Fahrenheit)

    °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32

    Definition: Converts a temperature from the Celsius scale to the Fahrenheit scale. This is commonly used when comparing weather reports or scientific data from different regions.
    Example: Convert 20°C to Fahrenheit.
    °F = (20 × 9/5) + 32 = 36 + 32 = 68°F.

    Length Conversion (Meters to Feet)

    feet ≈ meters × 3.281

    Definition: Converts a length from meters (metric system) to feet (imperial system). One meter is approximately equal to 3.281 feet.
    Example: Convert a 100-meter dash to feet.
    feet ≈ 100 × 3.281 = 328.1 feet.

    Mass Conversion (Kilograms to Pounds)

    pounds ≈ kilograms × 2.205

    Definition: Converts mass from kilograms (metric system) to pounds (imperial system). This is useful for measurements in fitness, cooking, and shipping.
    Example: Convert a mass of 70 kg to pounds.
    pounds ≈ 70 × 2.205 = 154.35 pounds.
